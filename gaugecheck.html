Real-Time Rain Gauge Quality Control

This dashboard monitors live rainfall telemetry across the UK. It automatically executes a suite of deterministic spatial, temporal, and physical quality control checks, combining them with AI-powered contextual analysis to verify genuine rain and flag hardware faults (e.g. blocked funnels, leaking counter buckets, or stuck sensors).
Checks
AI assessment
Method
Gross error (GEC)
Catches physically impossible or extremely rare readings in a single 15-min slot.
≥ 60 mm: hard reject (QI = 0), beyond any credible UK 15-min total (UK 30-min record ≈80 mm). ≥ 35 mm: very suspicious (QI −0.50), around the T50–100 return level for most UK sites.
E.g. a reading of 65 mm is implausible, since even the most extreme documented UK convective events have not produced 15-min totals this high.
Range check (RC)
Flags readings that are climatologically unusual for the season, even if not physically impossible.
Warm season (Apr–Sep): ≥ 25 mm suspicious. Cold season (Oct–Mar): ≥ 15 mm suspicious. QI −0.25 if triggered. Thresholds approximate T20–50 return periods; per-station 30-year data is unavailable so UK-wide estimates are used.
E.g. 16 mm in January, which is plausible in intense frontal rain but sufficiently unusual to warrant scrutiny; other checks and the AI assessment will provide context.
Temporal consistency (TCC)
Detects an isolated spike out of all proportion with the recent reading history.
Triggers when the reading is ≥ 8× the median of the preceding 7 slots (1 hr 45 min) AND ≥ 5 mm absolute. QI −0.25 if triggered.
E.g. previous 7 slots averaged 0.5 mm, then 12 mm appears, representing a 24× ratio with no spatial or radar support.
Spatial consistency (SCC)
Detects statistical outliers within a 100 km domain using the median and median absolute deviation (MAD).
Computes a deviation index Di = |gauge − domain median| / MAD. Di > 4: weak outlier (QI −0.10); Di > 8: medium (QI −0.25); Di > 15: strong (QI −0.50). Requires ≥ 5 domain stations.
E.g. gauge reports 12 mm while the domain median is 0 mm and MAD is 0 mm, yielding Di = 24, a strong outlier. Isolated genuine convective events may also score here; the AI assessment provides contextual disambiguation.
Persistent zero
Identifies a gauge stuck at zero while surrounding gauges and radar confirm active rainfall (RCC false-zero).
Triggers when ≥ 80% of the last 6 hrs are zero, ≥ 3 neighbours are actively recording, and radar shows ≥ 2 mm. QI −0.75 if triggered.
E.g. a blocked funnel or frozen sensor during an active rain event, meaning the tipping bucket simply never tips.
Radar conformity (RCC)
Compares the gauge reading against the Met Office 1 km radar composite at the same location (RCC false-positive).
Triggers when the reading is ≥ 5 mm, radar QPE is < 0.5 mm at that point, and the gauge/radar ratio exceeds 15×. QI −0.25 if triggered.
E.g. gauge reports 9 mm, radar sees 0.02 mm, which is a 450× ratio. Even allowing for radar attenuation, this is implausible.
Drip/leak pattern
Detects a leaking or blocked tipping bucket producing near-constant low-amplitude tips over a sustained period, representing a telltale hardware fault signature (no direct paper equivalent).
Two triggers over a 6-hour (24-slot) look-back window: (1) Uniform drip where ≥ 18 of 24 slots non-zero, mean < 1.5 mm, and CV < 0.15 (highly uniform intensity). QI −0.25. (2) Stuck counter where ≥ 12 consecutive slots recording the same value to the nearest 0.1 mm. QI −0.50. Both are suppressed if neighbours are also consistently wet (widespread drizzle). Note: tipping buckets record in 0.1 mm increments, so short runs of identical low values (under 12 slots) are a normal resolution artefact during genuine light rain.
E.g. exactly 0.2 mm every slot for six hours with dry neighbours and no radar signal, since a blocked funnel drip rate is near-constant in a way real rain is not. By contrast, 8 consecutive 0.1 mm slots during a tapering frontal event is likely genuine post-frontal drizzle.
Accumulation anomaly
Catches systematic over-recording only visible across the full 24-hour picture (extended spatial check).
Triggers when the 24 hr gauge total is ≥ 5× the mean of ≥ 3 nearby neighbours (each with ≥ 5 mm, neighbour mean ≥ 3 mm). QI −0.25 if triggered.
E.g. gauge accumulates 130 mm while three nearby stations each record 20–25 mm over the same period.
Checks combine into a Quality Index (QI) from 0.0 (reject) to 1.0 (fully trusted).
● QI 0.50–0.99 (uncertain; one or more weak signals).
● QI < 0.50 (poor; multiple or severe failures).
When it runs

Any time at least one check fails, or the reading is ≥ 20 mm even if all checks pass. Re-assessed every 4 consecutive flags (~1 hr) to stay current during prolonged events. Skipped between refreshes to keep API costs low.

What data it receives
  • Station location, season and month
  • Latest reading and how long the gauge has been continuously flagged
  • Accumulation totals: 1 hr / 3 hr / 6 hr / 12 hr / 24 hr
  • Wet/dry spell context: last significant rain, current spell length, 24-hr peak slot
  • All 7 check results with full numeric detail, including ratios, slot counts, CV values, neighbour readings
  • Which checks are new this run vs persistent from previous runs
  • Met Office radar QPE at 5 km and 30 km radius around the station
  • UKV NWP 1-hr forecast accumulation at the station (treated as soft corroboration, as NWP point data commonly underestimates localised convective totals)
  • Regional wetness: how many stations within 50 km are recording any rain or heavy rain
  • Full 24-hr slot history: 96 × 15-min readings oldest to newest
  • 3 nearest neighbours: latest reading, 6-hr slot history, and UKV forecast at each location
What it's asked to do

Act as an independent UK rainfall QC expert. Weigh all available evidence and return one of three verdicts (Genuine, Uncertain, or Suspect) accompanied by a 2–3 sentence explanation shown in the station popup. It is explicitly told not to re-run the deterministic calculations, only to interpret the pre-computed results in context. For the drip/leak check specifically, it is instructed to apply extra caution: tipping-bucket resolution means short runs of identical low values are normal during genuine light rain, and a drip flag on the tail of a real event at an upland site should lean toward Uncertain or Genuine unless other evidence clearly contradicts real rainfall.

Why AI alongside deterministic checks?

Each check examines one signal in isolation. The AI can weigh conflicting evidence that no single threshold can resolve: a Spatial failure during a known narrow convective band looks very different from one during a region-wide dry spell. A Drip flag during widespread post-frontal drizzle at an upland station is not the same as one on a clear afternoon with dry neighbours and zero radar. The AI adds the interpretive layer that fixed thresholds cannot provide.

Methodology

The QC process follows the RainGaugeQC framework described in:

Ośródka, K., Otop, I., and Szturc, J.: Automatic quality control of telemetric rain gauge data providing quantitative quality information (RainGaugeQC), Atmos. Meas. Tech., 15, 5581–5597, 2022.
https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-15-5581-2022

Each check reduces a Quality Index (QI) from a starting value of 1.0. The reductions are taken directly from Table 1 of the paper where applicable. Combined QI < 0.50 is treated as poor quality.

GEC and RC thresholds are informed by UK extreme rainfall records (Wikipedia, verified 2026-05-21): 5-min record 32 mm (Preston, 1893); 30-min record 80 mm (Eskdalemuir, 1953); 60-min record 92 mm (Maidenhead, 1901). These constrain what is plausible for a 15-min gauge slot, which is roughly up to ~50 mm in the most extreme historic UK convective events. These records are also included verbatim in the AI assessment prompt.

UK adaptations
  • No dual-sensor TCC. The paper's temporal consistency check compares paired heated and unheated sensors at the same site. UK Environment Agency telemetry gauges are single tipping buckets, so this comparison is not possible. The temporal check is therefore limited to spike detection rather than sensor agreement.
  • No per-station climatology (RC). The paper derives Range Check thresholds from 30-year hourly observations per station (1986–2015). That dataset is not available here. UK-wide seasonal estimates are used instead (15 mm warm season / 10 mm cold season), approximating the 1% annual exceedance probability.
  • Single spatial domain (SCC). The paper uses one primary 100 km subdomain plus four offset 25 km subdomains for robustness. Only the primary domain is implemented here.
  • No terrain-dependent RCC. The paper varies the radar conformity QI reduction by altitude zone (lowland / foothill / mountain). A flat deduction of 0.25 is used instead; no elevation data is incorporated.
  • Radar quality (QIR). The paper uses a formal radar quality index to weight radar-based checks. The Met Office composite is used here as a binary quality indicator only.
